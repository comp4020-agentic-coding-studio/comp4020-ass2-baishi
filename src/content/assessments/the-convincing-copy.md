## The brief

> Make a convincing copy of a real style or technique — in paint, prose,
> currency design or code — and write the note that would turn it from a
> forgery back into a copy.

Pick one material the studios have covered by week 6 (paint and physical
materials from weeks 1–5 are the most developed by this point, but a
handwriting or textual copy from week 5 is equally valid) and one real
source to study closely: a specific painter's brushwork, a specific writer's
sentence rhythm, a specific note's security features, a specific
programmer's coding style. The craft is getting close enough that the
connoisseur's-eye checklist from week 2 has to work to find the seam. The
judgement being tested is what you chose to get right first when you
couldn't get everything right at once — the same asymmetry week 2's lecture
named, now from the forger's side of it instead of the detective's.

## What you submit

The copy itself (a physical object photographed, a text file, a rendered
image, or a short audio/video clip, whichever fits your material), and a
materials-and-method note of 150–300 words: what real source you studied,
what you made the copy with, and which one decision you're least confident
survives scrutiny. A submission without the note is treated as undeclared
regardless of how good the copy is — see the [policies page](/policies/) for
why that line matters here specifically.
