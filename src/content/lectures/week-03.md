An eye can be trained; a canvas can be aged by hand; a signature can be
practised until it's indistinguishable from the original. What's much harder
to fake is chemistry that didn't exist yet. Titanium white wasn't a commercial
pigment before 1916 — a "seventeenth-century" painting containing it isn't a
matter of expert opinion, it's a matter of mass spectrometry. This lecture is
the materials-science half of detection: pigment and paper dating, the
radiocarbon problem for anything organic, and why the most damning evidence
against a forgery is usually the most boring — not a clumsy brushstroke, a lab
result.

## Outline

- anachronistic materials: the Vermeer forgeries that timed themselves out
