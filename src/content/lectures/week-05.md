A signature is the smallest forgery most people ever attempt, and the
easiest to get caught at, because a genuine signature is fast and
under-thought while a forged one is slow and over-controlled — the very care
that makes it convincing to the eye is what makes it wrong under a stroke
analysis. Literary and documentary forgery scales the same problem up: the
Hitler diaries fooled a magazine and several historians before a single
material test (that pigment problem again — a brightener not manufactured
until after the war) ended it in an afternoon. This lecture is about the
layer above materials: the stylistic and linguistic fingerprints a writer
leaves that are much harder to imitate deliberately than to produce by
accident.

## Outline

- stroke dynamics: why a forged signature is written slower than a genuine one
- the Hitler diaries and the Hughes autobiography: two forgeries that survived
