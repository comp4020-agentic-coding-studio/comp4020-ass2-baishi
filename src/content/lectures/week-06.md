No object on earth has had more design attention paid to defeating its own
forgery than a banknote, and every feature that makes a modern note strange to
look at — the microprinting, the colour-shifting ink, the raised intaglio you
can feel with a fingernail, the deliberately uneven serial-number kerning — is
a scar from a specific historical attack. Read a note's security features in
order and you're reading a hundred-year argument between engravers and
forgers, each move answered by the next. It's the cleanest example the
semester has of a general principle: a defence designed against a known attack
only holds until the attack changes, and the attacker always moves first.

## Outline

- a short history of note design as an arms race, feature by feature
- the counterfeiter's asymmetry: the state must defend every note, the forger
  only needs one to pass
- what banknote security teaches about designing any anti-forgery system,
  including a digital one
