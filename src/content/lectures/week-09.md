A generative model producing a convincing face or voice hasn't learned
"reality" — it's learned the statistical texture of its training data
convincingly enough to satisfy a viewer who isn't looking for anything in
particular. That's the same asymmetry from week 2's connoisseurship lecture,
run at industrial scale and without a human forger's own attention or
fatigue: a model doesn't get tired of getting every detail right, but it also
doesn't understand which details a specific viewer might check. Naledi Osei,
who builds detection tooling for broadcasters and archives, opens the
semester's two-week move from physical materials into pixels — same
questions about technique and tell, now asked of a system that never held a
brush.

## Outline

- what a diffusion or GAN model is actually optimising for, in one sentence
- where synthetic media still fails in ways a physical forger wouldn't (or
  would, for different reasons)
- the archive's problem: detecting at scale, not one suspicious object at a
  time
- this week's studio begins Assignment 3, "The Tell"
