Everything the semester has covered so far treats authenticity as a question
of fact — is this the real one. Markets treat it as a question of price, and
the gap between the two is where most of the real money in forgery actually
sits: an attributed work is worth a multiple of an identical, unattributed
one, which means the incentive to forge attribution (not just the object) is
enormous, and largely legal until someone can prove otherwise. Insurance,
auction-house due diligence and the entire authentication-services industry
exist to price a claim that's much harder to verify than most financial
claims, which is exactly why the final project asks for a provenance file
convincing enough to move a price, not just convincing enough to fool an eye.

## Outline

- why attribution changes price independently of the object
- authentication as a service industry: what auction houses and insurers

- the perverse incentive: forging paperwork is cheaper and safer than forging
